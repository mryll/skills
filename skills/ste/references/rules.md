# ASD-STE100 Issue 9 — Distilled writing rules

Condensed from the official specification (Issue 9, 2025-01-15), free download at
https://www.asd-ste100.org. This is a working summary — when in doubt, check the
cached spec text (see SKILL.md for the cache path).

## Section 1 — Words

- 1.1: Use only words that are approved in the dictionary, technical nouns, or technical verbs.
- 1.2: Use approved words only as their specified part of speech (dictionary approves "DISPLAY (n)" but not "display (v)").
- 1.3: Use approved words only with their approved meaning ("close" = verb only; use "near" for proximity).
- 1.4: Use only the approved forms of verbs and adjectives given in the dictionary.
- 1.5: A word not in the dictionary is usable if it fits one of the 22 numbered technical noun categories (full list at the end of this file). Label it TN with its category number.
- 1.6: Use an unapproved word only when it is a technical noun or part of one.
- 1.7: Do not use technical nouns as verbs ("do not oil the valve" → "apply oil to the valve").
- 1.8: Use the technical nouns of your company, industry, or subject field.
- 1.9: When you can choose a technical noun, pick the short, easy one.
- 1.10: No regional, slang, or jargon words as technical nouns.
- 1.11: One item = one technical noun, always the same one. Never vary terminology for style.
- 1.12: A verb not in the dictionary is usable if it fits one of the 4 numbered technical verb categories (full list at the end of this file). Label it TV with its category number.
- 1.13: Do not use technical verbs as nouns.
- 1.14: American English spelling (Merriam-Webster), unless an official directive says otherwise.

Issue 9 terminology: "technical noun" and "technical verb" (Issue 8 said "technical name" — do not use the old term).

## Section 2 — Multi-word nouns

- 2.1: Multi-word nouns: maximum three words.
- 2.2: If a technical noun has more than three words, write it in full once, then either give a shorter form or hyphenate the words that act as one unit ("pressure-relief valve").

## Section 3 — Verbs

- 3.1: Use only the verb forms given in the dictionary.
- 3.2: Only these forms/tenses: infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple future, past participle as adjective.
- 3.3: The past participle is usable only as an adjective.
- 3.4: No complex constructions with auxiliaries — no perfect tenses, no progressive, no "should/would/could" chains.
- 3.5: The "-ing" form only inside a technical noun or as its modifier ("landing gear" yes; "before commencing" no).
- 3.6: Active voice. In descriptive writing only, passive is allowed when the agent is unknown.
- 3.7: Describe an action with an approved verb, not a nominalization ("make a test" per dictionary style is fine; "perform the installation of" is not — write "install").

## Section 4 — Sentences

- 4.1: Write short and clear sentences.
- 4.2: Do not omit words or use contractions to shorten sentences. No telegraphic style.
- 4.3: Use a vertical list for complex texts (three or more items, conditions, or steps).
- 4.4: Use connecting words/phrases ("thus", "then", "but") to connect sentences with related topics.
- 4.5: Use an article (the, a, an) or a demonstrative adjective (this, these) before nouns whenever applicable.

## Section 5 — Procedural writing

- 5.1: Maximum 20 words per sentence. Warnings and cautions must also obey this.
- 5.2: One instruction per sentence, unless two or more actions occur at the same time.
- 5.3: Instructions in the imperative (command) form.
- 5.4: A condition the reader must know first goes BEFORE the command, separated with a comma ("When you supply hydraulic pressure, make sure that...").
- 5.5: Notes give information only, never instructions.

## Section 6 — Descriptive writing

- 6.1: Give information gradually — one subject per sentence.
- 6.2: Repeat key words and key phrases to give the text a logical structure.
- 6.3: Maximum 25 words per sentence.
- 6.4: Use paragraphs to group related information.
- 6.5: One topic per paragraph.
- 6.6: Maximum six sentences per paragraph.
- Imperative form is not permitted in descriptive text.

## Section 7 — Safety instructions

- Definitions: a WARNING signals risk of injury or death; a CAUTION signals risk of damage to objects.
- 7.1: Open with the word that identifies the level of risk (WARNING / CAUTION).
- 7.2: Start the safety instruction with a clear and accurate command or condition.
- 7.3: Then give an explanation that shows the risk or possible result.
- Shape: risk word → command/condition → consequence. All sentences ≤20 words.

## Section 8 — Punctuation and word count

- 8.1: All standard English punctuation is allowed EXCEPT the semicolon (;).
- 8.2: Hyphens connect words that are directly related.
- 8.3: Parentheses are allowed for references, item numbers, abbreviations, singular/plural forms, explanations, and alternatives.
- 8.4: In a vertical list, a colon ends a sentence for word-count purposes.
- 8.5: Text in parentheses counts as one word.
- 8.6: Each of these counts as one word: numbers, numbers with units, abbreviations, alphanumeric identifiers, quoted text, titles/headings/placards/labels, proper nouns.
- 8.7: Hyphenated words count as one word.

## Section 9 — Writing practices

- 9.1: When a word-for-word replacement is not enough, restructure the sentence.
- 9.2: Use each approved word correctly (check the dictionary examples).
- 9.3: No phrasal verbs — combinations whose meaning differs from their parts ("put out the fire" → "extinguish the fire"). Compositional verb + adverb is fine: the dictionary itself gives "GO BACK" as the alternative for "return".
- 9.4: Consistent style: same terminology, same wording, same construction for the same thing.

## General recommendations

- GR-1: Keep the conjunction "that" after verbs like "make sure", "show", "recommend" — never omit it.
- GR-2: Re-read every sentence with "with" — it is a known ambiguity source. Restructure if unclear, but keep the primary action verb ("Seal the opening with tool TS9867", not "Use tool TS9867 to seal...").
- GR-3: If a pronoun could refer to more than one noun, replace it with the noun.
- GR-4: The pronoun "this" must have an unmistakable referent — if not, repeat the noun or add the context again.
- GR-5: Watch false friends (English "disposition" is not Spanish "disposición" = instruction).
- GR-6: No Latin abbreviations (e.g., i.e., etc.) — write "for example", "that is", "or other ...".
- GR-7: Gender-neutral language always. "He"/"she" are not in the dictionary and not permitted.
- GR-8: The possessive ('s) is permitted but use it only when you are sure it is correct; otherwise restructure with "of".

## Technical noun categories (rule 1.5, Issue 9)

- 1: Official parts information
- 2: Vehicles or machines, and locations on them
- 3: Tools and support equipment, their parts, and locations on them
- 4: Materials, consumables, and unwanted material
- 5: Facilities, infrastructure, and logistic procedures
- 6: Systems, components and circuits, their functions, configurations, and parts
- 7: Mathematical, scientific, engineering terms, and formulas
- 8: Navigation and geographic terms
- 9: Numbers, units of measurement and time (and their symbols)
- 10: Quoted text
- 11: Professional roles, individuals, groups, organizations, and geopolitical entities
- 12: Parts of the body
- 13: Common personal effects, food, and beverages
- 14: Medical terms
- 15: Official documents, parts of documentation, standards, and guidelines
- 16: Environmental and operational conditions
- 17: Colors
- 18: Damage terms
- 19: Computer science, information and communication technology
- 20: Civil and military operations
- 21: Law and regulations
- 22: Animals, plants, and other life forms

## Technical verb categories (rule 1.12, Issue 9)

- 1: Manufacturing processes
- 2: Computer processes and applications
- 3: Instructions and information for applicable subject fields
- 4: Law and regulations
